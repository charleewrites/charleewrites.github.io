Urgent protective intervention guide: suspected elder financial abuse

Anonymised and edited portfolio version. All names, addresses, dates of birth, contact details and case-specific identifiers have been removed or generalised. Not legal advice.

Purpose: This sample demonstrates practical advocacy, procedural literacy, evidence organisation, plain-English drafting, and urgent risk mapping in a high-stakes administrative/legal context.

2. Case context

An older person was believed to be subject to financial risk after a family member acting under an Enduring Power of Attorney was said to be progressing the sale of the older person’s home. The family also raised concerns about restricted contact, lack of financial transparency, possible misuse of care-related funds, and uncertainty about the older person’s current wishes and decision-making capacity.

The immediate concern was not to prove every allegation at the first step. The immediate concern was to preserve the position long enough for proper review by the appropriate oversight bodies.

Central risk: the property could be sold, and sale proceeds could become difficult to trace or recover, before capacity, authority and financial management concerns were independently reviewed.

3. Strategic objective

The practical objective was to slow, pause or prevent irreversible action while creating a clear paper trail and escalating the matter to appropriate protective channels.

  • Preserve evidence: collect documents, messages, records, witness accounts and sale information.
  • Notify the correct bodies: raise the matter with the relevant public advocate/guardianship and tribunal pathways.
  • Disrupt the sale process lawfully: place the selling side on written notice that authority and instructions were disputed.
  • Seek urgent protective intervention: request review, production of records, and interim protection of the asset pending proper assessment.

4. Four-track action model

Track Action
Track 1: Preserve evidence Build a central evidence folder. Collect EPA documents, property listing records, care placement information, financial concerns, contact restriction evidence, witness statements and correspondence logs.
Track 2: Notify oversight bodies Prepare a concise complaint/referral pack for the relevant public advocate/elder abuse pathway and tribunal registry. Keep the first filing focused on urgency, capacity, authority and asset protection.
Track 3: Place sale parties on notice Send a formal written notice to the real estate agent and, if known, settlement/conveyancing parties. Request that no contracts, settlement steps or sale activity proceed while authority is disputed and urgent review is being sought.
Track 4: Seek protective orders Ask the tribunal or relevant decision-maker to consider interim protection, review of the attorney’s authority, production of financial records, and appointment of an alternative decision-maker if required.

5. Urgent execution plan

  1. Create a master folder. Separate material into evidence categories such as EPA documents, property sale, care placement, financial records, witness statements and correspondence.
  2. Prepare a one-page chronology. Record key dates: authority granted, contact restrictions, care changes, property listing, alleged payment demand and any urgent sale activity.
  3. Gather core documents. Prioritise documents that prove authority, sale activity, care placement, finances, payments, rent, utility records and communications.
  4. Prepare witness statements. Each person should state what they personally saw, heard or received. Avoid assumptions.
  5. Finalise the public advocate complaint. Attach the chronology and key supporting records.
  6. Prepare tribunal material. Include a cover letter, supporting statement, chronology and clearly labelled annexures.
  7. Send formal notice to the selling agent. Request preservation of documents and pause of sale steps pending review.
  8. Send notice to settlement or conveyancing parties if known. Keep proof of sending.
  9. Keep everything in writing. Follow up phone calls with same-day written summaries.
  10. Track responses. Use a response log showing recipient, date sent, document sent, response received and next action needed.
  11. Escalate if the sale moves quickly. If there is a live offer, contract preparation or settlement movement, immediately update the oversight bodies.
  12. Keep the message consistent. The focus is protection, review, records and preservation of the asset, not family conflict.
  13. Avoid unsupported claims. Use language such as “suspected misuse”, “concerns regarding” and “allegations requiring investigation” unless direct proof exists.
  14. Continue building the evidence pack. After the urgent referral, collect bank records, aged-care statements, tenancy records, rebate records and care placement documents.
  15. Prepare for next-stage process. Expect requests for more documents, a hearing or urgent conference, responses from the attorney, and possible legal representation.

6. Capacity and authority pathways

A key part of the document was separating the response depending on whether the older person had legal decision-making capacity at the relevant time.

Scenario Practical pathway
If the older person has capacity Obtain independent medical and/or legal confirmation of capacity. If the person understands the property, the EPA and the proposed sale, they may revoke the EPA, appoint a new attorney, and instruct the agent or settlement parties directly.
If the older person does not have capacity Act through protective channels. Seek public advocate/tribunal review, request urgent interim protection, ask for production of records, and seek appointment of an independent administrator or suitable substitute decision-maker if required.

8. Edited sample extracts

8.1 Urgent complaint/referral extract

We write on behalf of concerned family members regarding Older Person A. The family seeks urgent review and assistance because Older Person A’s major asset is at risk of being sold before the person’s current wishes, decision-making capacity, financial circumstances and attorney conduct have been independently examined.

The family’s immediate concern is that the property may be sold and sale proceeds may become difficult to trace before appropriate oversight can occur. The family therefore requests urgent guidance about protective steps, review of the attorney’s authority, production of financial records and preservation of the asset pending assessment.

8.2 Tribunal cover letter extract

This application is brought due to serious concerns about the management of Older Person A’s financial affairs by Attorney B, and the urgent risk that Older Person A’s property may be sold before the circumstances can be properly investigated.

The family respectfully seeks urgent consideration of protective orders, including review of Attorney B’s authority, production of relevant financial records, and interim steps to prevent irreversible dealing with the property pending proper consideration by the Tribunal.

8.3 Real estate notice extract

We give formal notice that there is a serious dispute regarding the authority being relied upon to progress the proposed sale of the property. Urgent review is being sought through appropriate protective channels.

In those circumstances, the family requests that no further steps be taken to progress the sale, exchange contracts, proceed toward settlement, or destroy any records relating to instructions, authority, identity checks, agency documents, correspondence, file notes or conveyancing communications.

9. Evidence discipline and risk control

A major strength of the original document was that it did not encourage reckless accusations. It directed the family to distinguish between allegation, concern, observation and documentary proof.

  • Use “suspected misuse” unless documentary proof establishes misuse.
  • Use “concerns regarding” when the issue requires investigation.
  • Keep witness statements limited to personal knowledge.
  • Avoid broad character attacks or unsupported conclusions.
  • Prioritise dates, records, communications, documents and decisions.
  • Separate the person’s welfare from family conflict or personal grievance.
